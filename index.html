Additional Technical Projects

Project 1: Programmable Analog IC with Integrated Amplifier, Potentiometer & DAC

  • Overview: This project involved the complete design, verification, and physical implementation of a multi-mode mixed-signal integrated circuit using the GPDK045 CMOS process in Cadence Virtuoso. The device integrates four selectable operating modes: a programmable inverting amplifier, programmable non-inverting amplifier, 16-tap digital potentiometer, and 4-bit buffered digital-to-analog converter (DAC). A 2-to-4 digital decoder provides mode selection, while shared analog circuitry allows the integrated operational amplifier and resistor network to be reconfigured for different functions.
  • Skills Learned:
    • Cadence Virtuoso & transistor-level CMOS circuit design
    • Operational amplifier & feedback circuit design
    • Digital potentiometer & transmission-gate array design
    • Digital decoder & mode-control implementation
    • CMOS integrated circuit layout
    • Schematic simulation & design verification
  • Resources Used: Cadence Virtuoso, GPDK045 CMOS process design kit, Cadence Schematic L, Layout XL, ADE Assembler, Cadence Genus, and Cadence Innovus.
  • Analysis & Results:
    • Designed a 16-tap digital potentiometer with a target impedance of 500 Ω between adjacent taps across the resistor string.
    • Implemented programmable inverting and non-inverting amplifier modes with 16 discrete gain settings, targeting maximum gains of 15 and 16, respectively.
    • Reconfigured the digital potentiometer as a precision resistor string for a 4-bit buffered DAC using a 1 V reference, targeting output levels from 0 to 0.9375 V.
    • Integrated the analog functions with a 2-to-4 digital decoder for user-selectable operating modes.
    • Completed schematic verification and physical layout while addressing implementation challenges.

Project 2: 5-Element Yagi-Uda Antenna Design

  • Overview: This project involved the complete electromagnetic design, element thickness compensation, and CST Studio Suite simulation of a 5-element Yagi-Uda antenna array, comprising one reflector, one driven element, and three directors—tuned to a 50.1 MHz resonant frequency (λ = 5.988 m). I modeled the full array geometry in CST Studio Suite while incorporating parasitic element thickness compensation (d/λ = 0.00424). To resolve feed-line matching challenges, I designed and integrated a folded dipole driving element to step up the input impedance for a direct match to a standard 50Ω port.
  • Skills Learned:
    • CST Studio Suite & MATLAB simulation
    • Yagi-Uda array synthesis & analytical formulations
    • Folded dipole design
    • S11 port impedance matching
    • 3D radiation pattern analysis & directivity optimization
    • Front-to-back ratio tuning & side-lobe level reduction
  • Resources Used: CST Studio Suite, Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design (Balanis), MATLAB, and analytical array formulations.
  • Analysis & Results:
    • Achieved a simulated directivity of ~10.6 dBi in CST.
    • Achieved an S11 return loss of -20 dB at 50.1 MHz, indicating excellent power transfer and input port matching.
    • The folded dipole provided a highly effective match to the 50Ω feed line, yielding a high front-to-back ratio and low side-lobe levels.

Project 3: 1.3 GHz Microwave Amplifier Design

  • Overview: This project focused on the design and simulation of a microwave amplifier optimized for maximum gain using Keysight ADS. The amplifier was designed around a BFR182 transistor biased at VCE = 8.0 V and IC = 10 mA, with a final design frequency of 1.3 GHz. The RF design used a combination of distributed microstrip transmission lines and lumped components for the bias-tee and impedance-matching networks. Stability networks were also incorporated to achieve unconditional stability across a wide frequency range while preserving gain at the design frequency.
  • Skills Learned:
    • Microwave amplifier design and S-parameter analysis
    • Microstrip transmission-line modeling and distributed RF networks
    • Distributed bias-tee, matching, and stability network design
    • RF PCB layout and practical component selection
    • Momentum electromagnetic and circuit co-simulation
    • Layout-aware RF design and parasitic analysis
  • Resources Used: Keysight ADS and Momentum electromagnetic simulation, Murata RF components, microstrip transmission-line models, and Microwave Engineering by David M. Pozar.
  • Analysis & Results:
    • Implemented distributed microstrip transmission-line networks for the input/output matching and bias-tee, using lumped Murata components where advantageous for physical layout.
    • Developed stability networks using resistive elements and frequency-dependent components whose self-resonant behavior produced effective shorts or opens at selected frequencies, improving stability while minimizing their impact on gain at 1.3 GHz.
    • Designed the RF circuitry with the physical PCB layout in mind, accounting for transmission-line geometry, component placement, and parasitic effects.
    • Used Momentum EM/circuit co-simulation in Keysight ADS to evaluate the interaction between the circuit schematic and physical layout.
    • Achieved unconditional stability from 10 MHz to 6 GHz while maintaining amplifier performance at the design frequency.
    • Achieved 11.2 dB gain at 1.3 GHz, exceeding our 10.6 dB target.
    • Achieved S11 and S22 below -20 dB at the design frequency, providing strong input and output matching.
