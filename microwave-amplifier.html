1.3 GHz Microwave Amplifier Design

This project focused on the design and simulation of a single-stage microwave amplifier optimized for maximum gain using Keysight ADS. The amplifier was designed around a BFR182 transistor biased at VCE = 8.0 V and IC = 10 mA, with a final design frequency of 1.3 GHz.

The RF circuitry combines distributed microstrip transmission lines with lumped Murata components for the bias-tee, dual input and output impedance-matching networks, and dual input and output stability networks. Layout-aware design principles and Momentum electromagnetic (EM) co-simulation were utilized to account for transmission-line geometry, component placement, and parasitic interactions.

02. Bias Tee Design

Distributed microstrip transmission-line networks paired with lumped Murata components designed to deliver stable DC bias (VCE = 8.0 V, IC = 10 mA) while isolating the RF signal path and minimizing parasitic loss.

Distributed and lumped bias-tee network schematic and modeling.

03. Initial Stability Analysis (Unstabilized Device)

S-parameter analysis of the active BFR182 transistor and bias network revealed region-dependent conditional instability (μ < 1) across lower RF frequencies. Without stabilization, presenting arbitrary source or load impedances risks severe out-of-band oscillation.

Initial stability factor (μ < 1) and source/load stability circles showing potentially unstable regions prior to stabilization.

04. Stability Network Design

Dedicated input and output stability networks incorporating resistive damping elements and self-resonant frequency-dependent components were implemented. This yields unconditional stability (μ > 1) from 10 MHz to 6 GHz without degrading transducer gain at the 1.3 GHz operating frequency.

Analysis & Results

  • Operating Point: BFR182 transistor biased at VCE = 8.0 V and IC = 10 mA at a design frequency of 1.3 GHz.
  • Initial vs. Final Stability: Unstabilized device exhibited conditional instability (μ < 1) at lower frequencies; addition of dual input and output stability networks established unconditional broadband stability (μ > 1) from 10 MHz to 6 GHz.
  • Gain Performance: Achieved 11.2 dB simulated transducer gain at 1.3 GHz, surpassing the 10.6 dB design target.
  • Impedance Matching: Tailored input and output microstrip matching networks brought S11 and S22 reflection coefficients below −20 dB at 1.3 GHz.
  • Physical Layout & Parasitics: Integrated microstrip geometries with Murata SMD components and verified layout interactions using Momentum EM/circuit co-simulation.
